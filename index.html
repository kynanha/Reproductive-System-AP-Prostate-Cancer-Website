The Disease

What Is Prostate Cancer?

Prostate cancer usually begins when the DNA inside prostate cells changes, or mutates. The cells stop following the body’s normal instructions for growth.

Healthy cells use cell-cycle checkpoints to control when they divide. Cancer cells can ignore these checkpoints and keep multiplying. They may form a malignant tumour and, in advanced cases, spread to other parts of the body. This spread is called metastasis.

Prostate cancer is most common in men over 50. Healthy habits during the teenage years—such as eating well, moving regularly, and avoiding smoking—can support long-term health.

#2 Most common cancer in males worldwide
1.4M+ New cases diagnosed per year
>99% 5-year survival if caught early

🫁 Urinary System

Urinary Bladder (Vesica Urinaria)

The urinary bladder is a stretchy, muscular organ that stores urine made by the kidneys. Two tubes called ureters carry urine into it.

🔗 Link to prostate cancer: A growing tumour can press on the bladder neck and urethra. This may make a person urinate more often or feel that the bladder has not emptied fully.
🟠 Primary Affected Organ

Prostate Gland (Glandula Prostatica)

The prostate gland is about the size of a walnut. It sits below the bladder and surrounds part of the urethra. It makes prostatic fluid, which protects and supports sperm and forms part of semen.

It has three main areas. Most prostate cancers begin in the outer area, called the peripheral zone.

🔗 How cancer starts: A DNA mutation can damage genes that normally control cell growth. The changed cells may then multiply too quickly and form a tumour.
💜 Reproductive System

Seminal Vesicles (Glandulae Vesiculosae)

The seminal vesicles are two small glands behind the bladder. They make much of the fluid in semen, including fructose that gives sperm energy.

🔗 Link to prostate cancer: In advanced cases, cancer cells can grow from the prostate into the nearby seminal vesicles. Doctors call this local spread.
🔵 Urogenital System

Urethra (Urethra Masculina)

The urethra is the tube that carries urine out of the body. In males, it also carries semen. Its first section passes through the prostate.

🔗 Link to prostate cancer: A tumour can squeeze the urethra. This may cause a weak urine stream, trouble starting, waking at night to urinate, or incomplete emptying.
Teenage Risk Factors

Habits That Raise Your Risk

Everyday habits can affect health over time. These examples show how diet, activity, body weight, smoking, and alcohol may change cancer risk.

🍔
High-Fat & Processed Diet
Diet / Nutrition

Eating lots of foods high in saturated fat, such as fast food and processed meat, may raise IGF-1, a hormone linked to cell growth. Too much growth signalling may help damaged cells survive and multiply.

HIGH
🎮
Sedentary Lifestyle
Physical Activity

Sitting for long periods and rarely exercising can increase chronic inflammation. Over time, inflammation may damage cells. Regular movement supports the immune system and helps the body control inflammation.

MOD–HIGH
⚖️
Obesity & Excess Body Fat
Body Composition

Extra body fat can change hormone levels and increase inflammation. Hormones such as insulin and leptin may send stronger growth signals to prostate cells, which can raise long-term risk.

HIGH
🚬
Smoking & Alcohol
Substance Use

Tobacco smoke contains carcinogens, which are chemicals that can damage DNA. Alcohol is broken down into acetaldehyde, another harmful chemical. Avoiding both protects cells from extra damage.

VERY HIGH
Biology of the Disease

How Your Habits Lead to Cancer

This simple pathway shows how repeated cell damage can sometimes lead to cancer over many years.

1

Harmful Habit

Unhealthy habits may expose the body to carcinogens, inflammation, or unbalanced hormones.

2

Oxidative Stress & Inflammation

Inflammation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) can damage DNA in prostate cells.

3

Oncogene Activation

Mutations may switch on growth genes and switch off tumour-suppressor genes that normally act like brakes.

4

Uncontrolled Proliferation

Damaged cells can ignore cell-cycle checkpoints, keep dividing, and form a tumour.

5

Metastasis

Cancer cells may enter blood or lymph vessels and spread to places such as bones or lymph nodes.

🔬

IGF-1 Signalling Pathway

IGF-1 is a hormone that tells cells to grow. Constantly high levels may give damaged prostate cells too many growth signals and make it harder for them to complete apoptosis, or planned cell death.

⚗️

Androgenic Stimulation (DHT)

The prostate responds to male hormones called androgens. One of them, DHT, attaches to receptors in prostate cells and gives growth instructions. Too much signalling can support abnormal growth.

🧬

Epigenetic Silencing

Epigenetic changes are chemical tags that affect how genes work without changing the DNA code. Some tags can silence tumour-suppressor genes, removing part of the cell’s safety system.

Warning Signs

Recognising the Symptoms

Early prostate cancer is often asymptomatic, meaning it causes no clear symptoms. Signs may appear as a tumour grows and presses on nearby organs.

💧

Urinary Dysfunction

Needing to urinate often or urgently, waking at night to go, or having a weak stream. These can happen when the prostate presses on the urethra.

⚡

Pelvic / Lumbar Pain

Ongoing pain in the pelvis, hips, or lower back can have many causes. In advanced prostate cancer, it may be linked to metastasis, or cancer spreading to bone.

🩸

Haematuria / Haematospermia

Blood in urine is called haematuria; blood in semen is called haematospermia. Either sign should be checked by a doctor.

🔴

Erectile Dysfunction

Difficulty getting or keeping an erection can happen if a tumour affects nerves and blood flow near the prostate.

😴

Unexplained Fatigue & Weight Loss

Advanced cancer can change how the body uses energy. This may cause unusual tiredness, loss of appetite, muscle loss, or weight loss.

🚿

Decreased Urinary Stream

A weaker urine flow, trouble starting, straining, or feeling that the bladder is still full may be signs of urethral obstruction.

What You Can Do Now

Prevention Starts Today

No habit can guarantee cancer prevention, but these healthy choices support the body and may lower long-term risk.

01
🍅

Put Colour On Your Plate

Real example: swap one packet of crisps for a bowl of watermelon, or ask for extra tomato sauce on your pasta. Tomatoes and watermelon carry lycopene, an antioxidant that helps protect cells from damage.

02
🥦

Eat Greens Twice A Week

Real example: add broccoli or cauliflower to a stir-fry, or grab the vegetable side at the canteen instead of fried food. These plants contain compounds that help cells repair their DNA.

03
🚴

Move About An Hour A Day

Real example: cycle or walk to school, play football at break, or do a 20-minute workout video after homework. Regular movement keeps insulin steady and lowers inflammation.

04
🥤

Cut The Sugary Drinks

Real example: make water your default bottle at school and keep fizzy drinks for weekends. Fewer sugar spikes means less extra body fat, and body fat affects hormone levels.

05
🚭

Never Start Smoking Or Vaping

Real example: plan one sentence you can actually say — "no thanks, I'm training." Smoke and vape aerosol carry carcinogens, chemicals that damage DNA inside cells.

06
😴

Sleep And Talk About Health

Real example: phone out of the bedroom for 8 hours of sleep, and tell your family what you learned here so older male relatives book a normal check-up with their doctor.
